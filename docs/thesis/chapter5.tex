\chapter{Experimental Results and Discussion}
\label{ch:results}

% Finding environment defined in main.tex preamble

This chapter presents experimental results across the three evaluation axes of the SustainRL-Bench framework introduced in \Cref{ch:methodology}: perturbation robustness (\Cref{sec:results_noise}), safe reinforcement learning (\Cref{sec:results_saferl}), and a consolidated cross-axis discussion (\Cref{sec:results_discussion}). Multi-agent RL results will be presented in \Cref{sec:results_marl} upon completion of ongoing experiments. For each axis, we report results across all three environments---EV Charging, Building, and Cogeneration---highlighting both positive findings and negative results, addressing the research questions posed in \Cref{sec:research_questions}.

\paragraph{A note on figure axes.} The training progress figures in this chapter use different x-axis units depending on the RL framework. Standard RL training curves (SB3) plot \emph{training episodes}, as episode reward is the natural monitoring unit for on-policy and off-policy algorithms. Safe RL training curves (OmniSafe) plot \emph{training epochs}, where each epoch corresponds to a fixed number of environment steps ($\text{steps\_per\_epoch} = 2000$). The test-time evaluation figures (violin plots, panel summaries) plot \emph{perturbation level} ($\sigma$) on the x-axis. These different units reflect the natural output format of each framework and are not directly comparable across axes.

\section{Perturbation Robustness}
\label{sec:results_noise}

We evaluate the perturbation robustness of three standard RL algorithms---PPO, SAC, and TD3---trained under clean conditions and tested under state perturbation, action perturbation, and dynamics perturbation injected at varying intensities (see perturbation injection framework in \Cref{sec:noise_framework}). All models are trained for 9M steps (EVCharging, Building) or 3M steps (Cogen) following the training configurations in \Cref{sec:experimental_design}, and evaluated over 100 episodes per perturbation condition. Statistical summaries include mean, standard deviation, and 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals as described in \Cref{sec:stat_methods}.

Several environment-specific observations emerge from the baselines:

\paragraph{EV Charging.} A striking finding is that all RL algorithms underperform the simple Greedy heuristic ($8.65$) and MPC-3 controller ($8.51$). PPO achieves only $69\%$ of the Greedy baseline, while SAC and TD3 fall below the Random policy. This suggests that the EV Charging reward structure---which combines profit, carbon cost, and network violations---is difficult for general-purpose RL to optimize, and domain-specific heuristics capture the key operational patterns more effectively. The Offline Optimal oracle ($8.32$) is slightly below Greedy, likely because it optimizes a different LP objective than the RL reward.

\paragraph{Building.} PPO ($-32.10$) is the clear winner, outperforming Do-Nothing ($-59.81$) by $46\%$ and Random ($-219.27$) by $85\%$. This demonstrates that RL learns genuinely useful HVAC control beyond trivial strategies. The Do-Nothing and MPC baselines achieve identical performance, suggesting that the rule-based MPC controller defaults to zero action in this configuration. TD3's high variance ($\text{std} = 26.85$) indicates inconsistent HVAC control across episodes.

\paragraph{Cogeneration.} SAC and TD3 achieve near-zero reward ($\approx -0.03$ to $-0.04$), vastly outperforming Random ($-1.84$, $50\times$ worse) and Do-Nothing ($-7.36$, $200\times$ worse). PPO's conservative strategy ($-1.76$) is comparable to Random, confirming that PPO fails to learn effective plant dispatch despite achieving the lowest fuel cost (\$11,001 vs.\ \$18,700 for SAC).

\subsection{Building: Perturbation Robustness}
\label{sec:noise_bu}

The Building environment is the most perturbation-sensitive of the three environments, with rapid performance degradation under state perturbation and dynamics perturbation. The RC thermal model amplifies per-timestep observation errors through coupled zone dynamics, creating cascading failures over the 288-step episode.

% --- Building PPO ---
\subsubsection{Building --- PPO}

\paragraph{Training curves.} \Cref{fig:stdrl_bu_ppo_noise} shows a clear separation between perturbation levels during training: higher state perturbation leads to lower converged reward, and action and dynamics perturbation show similar but more severe training degradation patterns.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

\paragraph{Training curves.} TD3 exhibits a distinctive \emph{mid-training crash} in the Cogeneration environment (\Cref{fig:stdrl_co_td3_ds}): the baseline (perturbation $= 0$) converges to near-optimal performance ($\approx -0.05$) by episode 10,000, then catastrophically collapses to $-1.8$ around episode 15,000--20,000, before gradually recovering to $-0.7$ by the end of training. This U-shaped pattern is characteristic of TD3's value overestimation instability in environments with complex, nonlinear dynamics.

Paradoxically, perturbation-trained TD3 agents (state perturbation $\sigma = 10, 17, 25$) exhibit \emph{more stable} training than the clean baseline---they converge smoothly to $\approx -0.2$ without any mid-training collapse. The state perturbation acts as implicit regularization, preventing the Q-function from overfitting to narrow state-action regions and thus avoiding the catastrophic overestimation that destabilizes the clean-trained agent.

\paragraph{Test-time performance.} Despite the training instability, TD3's best model achieves competitive test-time performance ($-0.040 \pm 0.073$), comparable to SAC ($-0.033 \pm 0.079$). However, the training instability means that model selection (choosing the right checkpoint) is critical for TD3 in Cogeneration, whereas SAC provides more reliable convergence.

\begin{finding}[Environment-Dependent Perturbation Sensitivity (RQ1, RQ5)]
\label{find:noise_sensitivity}
Perturbation robustness varies dramatically across environments: EV Charging policies are exceptionally robust to all perturbation types ($<1\%$ degradation at moderate perturbation), while Building policies degrade rapidly under state and dynamics perturbation ($>100\%$ degradation at $\sigma \geq 0.15$). \begin{finding}[State vs.\ Action Perturbation Asymmetry (RQ2)]
\label{find:obs_act_asymmetry}
Across all environments, state perturbation causes significantly more degradation than action perturbation at equivalent scales. The practical implication is clear: for real-world deployment of RL-based controllers, sensor accuracy is more critical than actuator precision.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[Algorithm-Specific Robustness (RQ1)]
\label{find:algo_robustness}
PPO consistently demonstrates superior perturbation robustness compared to SAC and TD3, with the most dramatic difference in Cogeneration (PPO: $<0.3\%$ degradation; SAC: $>2000\%$ degradation). PPO's on-policy training and clipped surrogate objective create naturally conservative policies that tolerate input perturbations, while SAC's entropy-maximizing exploration can amplify perturbation in the policy output.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[TD3 Training Instability (RQ1)]
\label{find:td3_instability}
TD3 exhibits severe training instability across all three environments: monotonic performance decline in EV Charging, catastrophic degradation in Building (reward worsening from $-50$ to $-150$ over training), and a dramatic mid-training crash in Cogeneration. This instability is attributable to TD3's deterministic policy and value overestimation compounding over long training horizons with complex dynamics. Notably, in Cogeneration, state perturbation during training acts as \emph{implicit regularization} that stabilizes TD3---perturbation-trained agents converge smoothly while the clean-trained agent crashes. This suggests that perturbation injection may serve a dual purpose: not only testing robustness but also improving training stability for off-policy deterministic algorithms.
\end{finding}

\section{Safe Reinforcement Learning}
\label{sec:results_saferl}

We evaluate four on-policy CMDP algorithms---PPO-Lagrangian (PPOLag), CPO, OnCRPO, and FOCOPS---under varying cost limits using the CMDP formulations described in \Cref{sec:safe_rl}. All models are trained for 6M steps (3000 epochs $\times$ 2000 steps/epoch) using the OmniSafe framework~\citep{ji2023omnisafe} with the hyperparameters specified in \Cref{sec:experimental_design}.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Cogeneration: Strong Constraint Differentiation}
\label{sec:saferl_cogen}

The Cogeneration environment provides the strongest Safe RL results. The CMDP cost---cumulative turbine ramping stress---is orthogonal to the reward, which is dominated by fuel and delivery costs. The reward's built-in ramp penalty ($w_{\text{ramp}} = 2$) is negligible, so the unconstrained agent ignores turbine stress entirely.

\Cref{fig:cogen_compare} compares all four algorithms at $d = 25$, a moderate cost limit that requires meaningful constraint enforcement. Several key observations emerge from the training dynamics:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PPOLag} achieves the best reward ($\approx -0.04$) while converging its cost to the $d = 25$ limit with high precision. The Lagrangian multiplier $\lambda$ provides smooth, adaptive constraint enforcement: when cost exceeds the limit, $\lambda$ increases to penalize unsafe behavior; when cost is below, $\lambda$ decreases to release reward capacity. This feedback loop produces the tightest constraint tracking among all algorithms.
    \item \textbf{CPO and OnCRPO} converge faster in cost reduction ($\sim$500 epochs vs.\ $\sim$1500 for PPOLag), driven by their trust-region updates that enforce per-step cost improvement guarantees. However, both algorithms overshoot the cost limit, converging to $\approx 12$--$15$ rather than $25$---sacrificing reward unnecessarily. The trust-region mechanism lacks the fine-grained $\lambda$-based control that allows PPOLag to settle precisely at the constraint boundary.
    \item \textbf{FOCOPS} exhibits the slowest convergence, requiring $\sim$2000 epochs to stabilize. Its first-order constraint optimization produces noisy cost trajectories with wide confidence bands, though it eventually reaches a cost of $\approx 17$, closer to the limit than CPO/OnCRPO.
\end{itemize}

Across all four cost limits ($d \in \{10, 25, 50, 200\}$), PPOLag is the only algorithm that produces monotonically increasing final costs ($5, 12, 23, 43$) matching the prescribed limits, confirming that Lagrangian relaxation is uniquely suited for precise safety--performance tradeoff control in this environment. The algorithm ranking at $d = 25$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \text{PPOLag} \succ \text{OnCRPO} \approx \text{CPO} \succ \text{FOCOPS}
\end{equation}

\Cref{fig:building_compare} presents the cross-algorithm comparison at $d = 25$, which visually confirms the failure of CMDP constraint differentiation and reveals additional pathological behaviors:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PPOLag} achieves the best reward ($\approx -25$) and reduces cost to $\approx 5$, well below the $d = 25$ limit. Because the constraint is trivially satisfied, $\lambda \to 0$ and PPOLag effectively degenerates to standard PPO. This graceful degradation is a strength: PPOLag does not suffer from inactive constraints.
    \item \textbf{CPO} exhibits severe reward divergence, collapsing to $\approx -90$ after $\sim$1500 epochs despite maintaining low cost. The trust-region update becomes numerically unstable when the cost constraint is nearly inactive: with no meaningful cost gradient to guide the trust region, the optimization direction becomes dominated by numerical noise, causing catastrophic policy degradation.
    \item \textbf{OnCRPO} shows a similar but delayed divergence pattern, with reward degrading to $\approx -50$ after $\sim$2000 epochs. The shared trust-region mechanism explains the similar failure mode, though OnCRPO's additional constraint relaxation step delays the onset of instability.
    \item \textbf{FOCOPS} maintains stable reward ($\approx -35$) throughout training but at a lower level than PPOLag, with noisy cost oscillations around $5$--$15$. The first-order Lagrangian approach avoids trust-region instability but produces noisier constraint tracking.
\end{itemize}

The root cause is a \emph{reward--cost alignment problem}: the reward function with $\beta = 0.5$ already strongly incentivizes both energy efficiency and thermal comfort. The agent minimizes temperature errors through reward optimization alone, trivially satisfying any temperature-based CMDP constraint. This finding has an important practical implication: CMDP formulations are only useful when the cost signal captures an objective that the reward function inadequately addresses. In the Building environment, the reward's comfort penalty ($\beta = 0.5$) already enforces thermal constraints, making the CMDP layer redundant.

\subsection{Off-Policy Safe RL: SACLag Failure}
\label{sec:saclag}

SACLag (SAC with Lagrangian relaxation) failed consistently across all three environments. The root cause is a fundamental incompatibility between replay buffer data staleness and Lagrange multiplier updates: the cost critic is trained on stale transitions, causing $\lambda$ to oscillate or diverge.

\subsection{Safe RL Findings}

\begin{finding}[Reward--Cost Orthogonality Is Necessary (RQ3)]
\label{find:orthogonality}
CMDP formulations require the cost signal to measure an objective not adequately incentivized by the reward. When reward and cost are aligned (Building: $\beta = 0.5$ comfort term), constraints are trivially satisfied. When orthogonal (Cogen: ramp stress; EV: excess charge), CMDP enables precise safety--performance tradeoff control.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[PPOLag Is the Most Robust CMDP Algorithm (RQ3)]
\label{find:ppolag_best}
Across all environments, PPOLag provides the best cost limit differentiation, precise constraint tracking, and graceful degradation under inactive constraints ($\lambda \to 0 \Rightarrow$ standard PPO). Trust-region methods (CPO, OnCRPO) converge faster but are vulnerable to numerical instability under loose constraints.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[Off-Policy CMDP Methods Fail (RQ3)]
\label{find:saclag}
SACLag's replay buffer staleness prevents accurate Lagrange multiplier tracking. This negative finding suggests that on-policy methods are currently the only reliable approach for CMDP-based Safe RL.
\end{finding}

\section{Cross-Axis Discussion}
\label{sec:results_discussion}

The results across perturbation robustness and Safe RL reveal several cross-cutting insights.

\subsection{Environment Characteristics Matter More Than Algorithm Choice}

The most consistent finding across both experimental axes is that environment structure---not algorithm hyperparameters---determines the effectiveness of robustness and safety interventions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{EV Charging} is inherently robust to perturbation (aggregate daily patterns are perturbation-resistant) and amenable to Safe RL (excess charge is orthogonal to profit).
    \item \textbf{Building} is inherently fragile to perturbation (thermal dynamics amplify errors) and resistant to Safe RL (reward already captures both objectives).
    \item \textbf{Cogeneration} shows algorithm-dependent perturbation sensitivity (PPO robust, SAC fragile) and strong Safe RL behavior (ramp stress is under-penalized in reward).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{PPO's Dual Advantage}

PPO emerges as the most robust algorithm across both axes: it tolerates perturbation better than SAC/TD3 (\Cref{sec:noise_ev,sec:noise_bu,sec:noise_co}) and provides the best CMDP constraint satisfaction as PPOLag (\Cref{sec:results_saferl}). This dual advantage stems from PPO's on-policy nature: conservative policy updates prevent both perturbation amplification and stale-data problems that plague off-policy methods.

\subsection{Implications for Sustainable Energy Systems}

The practical implications for real-world deployment are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sensor investment}: State perturbation is far more damaging than action perturbation, prioritizing sensor accuracy over actuator precision.
    \item \textbf{Safety constraint design}: CMDP constraints must measure genuinely under-optimized objectives; redundant constraints (already captured by reward) provide no benefit.
    \item \textbf{Algorithm selection}: PPO/PPOLag provides the best robustness--safety combination, despite SAC's superior clean-condition performance in some environments.
\end{enumerate}
